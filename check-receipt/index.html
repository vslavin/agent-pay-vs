Agent Pay

Check a receipt

A receipt says a person allowed an AI agent to buy something, and that the store accepted it and was paid. You do not have to take the store's word for that, or ours. This page re-checks the signatures itself, one link at a time, so you can see how a mandate holds the whole thing together.

Private by design. Everything you paste or load stays in this browser tab. This page only contacts a server when you press a button that says so (fetching a key, looking up a transaction).

What you can check, and with what

Every piece you add unlocks another link in the chain. You can stop at any point: whatever you have is checked, and the page says what is still missing.

The buyer's evidence bundle contains all of these. The public raw data link on a receipt page contains only the first row.

    What this page cannot tell you

    • Where a key came from. A signature proves that whoever holds a key signed something. Keys that arrive inside a bundle are only as trustworthy as the bundle. To check independently, get the store's key from the store's own catalog and Keycloak's keys from its public certificate address (the buttons in steps 4 and 6 do that).
    • That the sign-in was a passkey. It shows that the account signed in again, bound to the exact terms. The method (amr) is shown when Keycloak recorded it.
    • Whether the user's signing key was used only with consent. In this demo the wallet service holds it, and signs only after the sign-in above.
    • Which purchase mode the store ran. A bundle without an approval looks like an autonomous purchase; the bundle does not record the store's setting.
    • The total budget, or a revocation made later. One order cannot show how much else was spent, and revocation is not part of the evidence.

    The checks are about 400 lines in verify.js, with no libraries; it uses your browser's built-in cryptography. It is a second implementation of the checks in the wallet service, and a test requires the two to agree on every bundle, honest or tampered.
